Build an interactive, end to end simulation of a single Starlink satellite pass over a ground station, but model the spacecraft as hardware first and a network node second. Use a real Starlink TLE, then resolve every second of the pass into a high fidelity onboard hardware model: phased array beam steering, element level gain behavior, RF front end nonlinearities, power amplifier backoff, oscillator stability, frequency reference drift, thermal effects on radio performance, polarization behavior, pointing constraints, power bus limits, compute scheduling, and faulted or degraded component states. The satellite cannot behave like a perfect transmitter. Its RF output, beam shape, timing stability, and usable throughput must emerge from hardware constraints that evolve over the pass.

Then force those hardware realities all the way through the stack. The link budget, Doppler tracking, BER, adaptive modulation, packet loss, jitter, retransmissions, and goodput must all be downstream consequences of the spacecraft’s actual internal state, not independent curves. The UI should let us inspect the satellite at subsystem depth while also exposing packet sniffing style traces at the ground station, so that a throughput collapse can be traced back not just to low elevation, but to a specific hardware level cause such as phased array degradation, thermal throttling, timing drift, amplifier compression, or onboard resource contention. At any second, we should be able to explain a broken flow in terms of what the satellite’s hardware was doing internally.
